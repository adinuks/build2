# Value Creation

Creation moves from "here is the problem" to "here is what we could build". Your first idea is your assumption. Your tenth is your thinking. Your fifteenth is where it gets interesting. Most students arrive with a solution already chosen, so the work is to question it before building it.

## What do you want to achieve?

| I want to... | Do this | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Come up with solutions to my problem | Free ideation (10 ideas or more), SCAMPER, filter, pick one | `ref/ideate-conceptualize.md`, steps 0 to 6 |
| Turn my chosen idea into a concept and press release | Three-sentence concept, four-line press release, then a longer release and FAQ if needed | `ref/ideate-conceptualize.md`, steps 7 and 8 |
| Decide what to build and what to leave out | Value proposition, design direction, what it should and should not be, priorities, user stories | `ref/design-strategy.md` |

## Rules for every mode

- **Start from the Problem Statement.** Read `need-finding.md`. If there is no sharp Problem Statement, stop and run `value-thinking`. Ideas without a sharp problem are just brainstorming.
- **Quantity first, then choose.** Minimum ten ideas, fast, no filter. The obvious idea is usually not the right one. Then pick one: not the safest, not the most obvious, the one that best answers the Problem Statement.
- **Concept in plain language.** Three sentences, one each: What does it do? Who is it for? What changes for them because it exists? No jargon, no feature list.
- **Press release, four lines.** *Today we launched [product] for [who]. It helps them [do what] so that [outcome]. Before this, they had to [old painful way]. The first person who should use this is [specific description].* If any line cannot be written, the concept is not clear. Go back one step. Read line three aloud: if the old way does not sound painful, the real problem has not been found.
- **Strategy is the art of saying no.** A yes should come from elimination.
- **The student chooses.** You generate and organise. They decide.
- Plain language. Short sentences. No em dashes.

## Gate before moving on

- The chosen idea directly answers the Problem Statement and can be said in one sentence.
- The student genuinely does not know if it will work. If they are certain, push further.
- Someone who has never heard of it could read the press release and say who it is for and why it matters.

## Save and hand off

Write `concept-note.md` (and `design-strategy.md` if run). Add a three-line summary to `intent.md`: the chosen idea, the concept in one sentence, the press release line three. Then suggest `value-delivery`.
