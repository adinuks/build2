# Value Communication: LinkedIn Post

A post is the smallest unit of value communication. One idea, one audience, one breath.

Posting is externalization. A thought only becomes real when it leaves your head, and a project only builds your reputation when people can see it. One small, visible project a week, shared well, compounds into a personal brand that makes employers come to you.

The goal is credibility, not likes. Show the thinking, not just the result.

## Your role

- **The student decides the one idea.** You help them find it.
- **You draft in their voice, not yours.** If they share two or three past posts or messages, match them.
- **Every claim is real.** Never invent numbers, results, quotes, clients or reactions. Missing proof gets `[add number]` or the line goes.
- **Protect them.** Flag anything confidential: employer data, customer names, unreleased work, other people named without consent.
- Plain language. Short sentences. No em dashes. No exclamation marks unless the student uses them.

## Step 1: What are you sharing?

Ask:

1. **What happened, or what do you think?** The raw material. A project shipped, a case study, a lesson, a research insight, a decision, a mistake.
2. **Who should read it?** Hiring managers for a target role, peers, founders, students, customers.
3. **What should they think or do after?** See you as capable, learn one thing, try your prototype, reach out.

Read `prototype-notes.md`, `concept-note.md` or other stage files if the post is about a project. Pull the real details.

Then confirm the **one idea** in one sentence. If there are three ideas, there are three posts.

## Step 2: Pick the post type

| Type | Shape |
|---|---|
| **Build in public** | I built X for Y. Here is what I learned building it. |
| **Case study in brief** | The human, the insight, the decision, the outcome. |
| **Lesson learned** | What I believed, what happened, what I believe now. |
| **Research insight** | The surprising thing real people told or showed us. |
| **Behind the decision** | We chose A over B. Here is the trade-off. |
| **Point of view** | A clear stance, backed by experience. |

All of them follow the same spine as every value communication: start with a real human, connect to a real number, end with a clear takeaway.

## Step 3: Write the hook

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second. Only the first two lines or so show before "see more".

Draft **three hook options** with different strategies. The student picks one or rewrites.

- **Story:** drop into a moment. "Ten elders told us the pain wasn't the knee."
- **Insight:** lead with the surprising finding.
- **Tension:** a belief most people hold, and why the evidence disagrees.

Hook rules: short, specific, concrete. Surprise holds attention (that is dopamine's job). No clickbait, no "I'm thrilled to announce", no questions that beg for engagement.

## Step 4: Write the body

- **Short lines. White space.** One thought per line. Words plus space feel simple.
- **Problem, Agitation, Solution**, or **Situation, Action, Change** for a story.
- **Show the thinking:** the insight, the decision, the trade-off. This is what separates a builder from a poster.
- **One number** if you have a real one. A number beats an adjective.
- **Give before you ask.** The reader should learn something even if they never click.
- **Length:** usually 120 to 250 words. Shorter if the idea is sharp.

## Step 5: Close

- **Takeaway:** one line the reader can repeat.
- **Soft call to action** that matches Step 1: a link to the prototype or site, an invitation to talk, or nothing at all.
- **Hashtags:** zero to three, only if relevant.
- **Visual (optional):** suggest one real image the student has, such as a prototype screen, a sketch or a photo of the work. Never a fake screenshot.

## Step 6: Check

Run these before handing over:

- **Read-aloud test.** Does it sound like the student talking? Mark anything that does not.
- **One idea test.** Could a reader repeat the point in one sentence?
- **Proof test.** Is every claim real and every number theirs?
- **Thinking test.** Does it show how they think, not just what they did?
- **Safety test.** Anything confidential, unconsented or that an employer would object to?
- **Adjective test.** Replace "passionate", "amazing", "game-changing" with a fact, or cut them.

## Step 7: Hand over

Give the final post in the reply as plain text, ready to paste. Also save it as `post-[topic].md` so posts accumulate into a record of the student's thinking. Never overwrite earlier posts.

Add a short note: the one idea, the hook strategy used, and one idea for the next post that builds on this one.

If the student wants a series, suggest a weekly rhythm tied to their weekly project: build on the weekend, post on Monday.

## Self-check

- Is there exactly one idea?
- Would the first line make the right reader stop?
- Does it show my thinking, backed by something real?
- Does it sound like me?
