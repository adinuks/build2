# Audit question bank

Claude draws from this when preparing. Pick the questions that fit the level. Ask them as plain questions about a specific moment, never as jargon.

## Looks: what the eye does

**Visual perception principles.** Use them to spot the issue, then ask it as a plain question.
- **Hierarchy.** What is the first thing the eye lands on? Is it the right thing?
- **Contrast and emphasis.** What stands out? What should, and does not?
- **Proximity.** Do things that belong together sit together?
- **Similarity.** Do things that look alike act alike?
- **Alignment and rhythm.** Does the page feel ordered, or random?
- **Figure and ground.** Can you tell content from background at a glance?
- **Closure and continuity.** Does the eye follow a clear path down the page?
- **Legibility.** Size, weight and colour contrast of the words that matter.

**Nielsen heuristics that show up here:** 4 Consistency and standards. 6 Recognition rather than recall. 8 Aesthetic and minimalist design.

Sample questions:
- "On this screen, what did your eye go to first? Is that what matters most?"
- "Which two things look like they belong together but sit apart? Or the reverse?"
- "Is there anything here you could remove and lose nothing?"

## Works: what the mind does

**Cognitive principles.**
- **Attention.** Is the person's attention pointed at the next step?
- **Cognitive load.** How many things must they hold in mind at once? 
- **Recognition over recall.** Do they see the options, or must they remember them?
- **Choice.** More options means slower decisions. Are there too many here?
- **Mental model.** Does it behave the way the person expects from the real world and other products?
- **Signifiers.** Does the thing that can be tapped look tappable?
- **Feedback.** After an action, does the product answer?
- **Effort.** Count the steps and the taps to finish the task.

**Nielsen heuristics that show up here:**
1. Visibility of system status.
2. Match between the system and the real world.
3. User control and freedom.
5. Error prevention.
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use.
9. Help users recognise, diagnose and recover from errors.
10. Help and documentation.

Sample questions:
- "After you tapped this, how did you know it worked?"
- "Where would you tap to go back? Did you find it?"
- "What did you have to remember from the last screen to use this one?"
- "If you made a mistake here, what happens? Could you recover?"

## Hooks: what the person feels and what it means to them

Reflective perception is about meaning, trust, identity and memory. It is what stays with the person after they close it. The student answers as the person in the Desire Profile.

Draft question set. Karthi to edit or replace with her own interview questions.
1. At what moment did you feel most confident? At what moment did you feel unsure?
2. What did you expect to happen next? Did it?
3. Did you trust it with what you gave it? What made you trust or doubt it?
4. What does using this say about you? Would you be happy to be seen using it?
5. How did you feel when you finished?
6. What almost made you quit?
7. Why would you come back tomorrow? What would bring you back?
8. What would you tell a friend about it, in one sentence?

At the business level, add: "What would make you pay for this? What would make you stop paying?"

Record the answer in the student's own words. Do not paraphrase it into something it was not.
